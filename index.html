GHRP-6
the hunger hexapeptide

The original synthetic hunger-and-growth-hormone peptide: six amino acids that impersonate ghrelin, the "I'm starving" hormone — triggering a growth-hormone pulse and, famously, a ravenous appetite. Studied for decades, approved nowhere, banned in sport.

Quick read

GHRP-6 is a six-amino-acid peptide that mimics ghrelin, the stomach hormone that signals hunger. It docks on the ghrelin receptor (GHS-R1a) in the brain and pituitary, triggering a pulse of growth hormone — and, just as reliably, a wave of intense hunger. First made by university researchers in the 1980s, it reliably raises GH in human lab measurements, but it was never approved as a medicine and remains a research chemical, banned in sport. 01The hexapeptide

What GHRP-6 actually is

GHRP-6 is the founding member of the growth-hormone-releasing peptides: a tiny six-amino-acid chain that fools the brain into thinking ghrelin just arrived. Everything sold as GHRP-6 is an unregulated research chemical.

Six amino acids in a row. The mirror-image 'D' forms are what keep enzymes from chewing it up instantly.

A ghrelin impersonator

GHRP-6 mimics ghrelin, the stomach hormone that tells the brain you're hungry. It fits the same receptor (GHS-R1a) — which is why one molecule causes two very different effects: a GH pulse and hunger.

The original GHRP

The first synthetic growth-hormone-releasing peptide, made in the 1980s. Every later GHRP — GHRP-2 and hexarelin — is a variation on this theme, tuned for more GH and less hunger.

Research chemical only

GHRP-6 has no approved medical use anywhere. What's sold online is unregulated: no quality oversight, no purity guarantees, no approved label.

Plain English

Ghrelin is your stomach's 'feed me' text message to the brain. GHRP-6 is a forged text from the same number — the brain believes it, releases growth hormone, and also orders lunch. You can't get the pulse without the appetite; they're the same message.

02The 1980s origin

Born in a university lab

Before anyone knew what ghrelin was, researchers were already building molecules that acted like it. GHRP-6 came first — and its discovery helped reveal the hunger hormone itself.

The molecule came before its own explanation — researchers built a ghrelin mimic years before ghrelin was discovered.
A molecule before its receptor

GHRP-6 was made before anyone knew the ghrelin receptor existed. Researchers found that certain small peptides released GH, and only later did the 'why' arrive: a whole hunger-hormone system.

Decades of lab work

Universities studied GHRP-6 for decades — GH pulses, appetite, metabolism. It became a standard research tool for probing the ghrelin system, not a drug candidate.

The template for everything after

GHRP-2 and hexarelin are both descendants of this hexapeptide, each tweaked to keep the GH pulse while dialing the hunger (and other effects) up or down.

Plain English

GHRP-6 is the Model T of growth-hormone peptides: historically huge, scientifically foundational — and nobody drives one anymore if they can get a newer model. Its job in history is secure; its job in medicine never arrived.

04Evidence check

What the science actually supports

GHRP-6 has more human data than most research peptides — and still no evidence it helps anyone. Both facts fit in one paragraph.

GH pulses: demonstrated

In controlled human experiments, GHRP-6 reliably triggers growth-hormone release. This is real, replicated human pharmacology — not animal extrapolation.

Hunger: demonstrated

Increased appetite and food intake are documented effects, not forum folklore. If anything, the studies understate how strong the hunger can feel.

Benefits: none shown

No trial has tested GHRP-6 for muscle, fat loss, aging, or recovery in a way that shows a real outcome. The evidence file ends at 'it moves hormones.'

Two effects are rock-solid in the lab; every benefit claim beyond them is unsupported.
Plain English

GHRP-6 is unusual: the drug works exactly as advertised — pulse, hunger, the lot. What's missing isn't pharmacology, it's any reason to want those effects outside a laboratory.

06Risks & unknowns

Risks & unknowns

Decades of research mean the short-term effects are well mapped. The long-term picture in healthy users is still blank.

Banned in sport

Observed fact: WADA prohibits GHRPs including GHRP-6 (S2 category). Athletes have been sanctioned — the ban is real regardless of the evidence debate.

Unregulated vials

Observed fact: gray-market GHRP-6 has no quality oversight. Independent testing of research peptides has repeatedly found wrong doses, impurities, and sometimes the wrong compound entirely.

Hunger and weight

Plausible: a drug that reliably triggers intense hunger can drive overeating and fat gain — the opposite of what most users want. The effect is the risk.

Prolactin and cortisol

Observed in studies: GHRP-6 raises prolactin and cortisol more than newer GHRPs. Chronically elevated levels could matter — unquantified in casual use.

Long-term effects

Unknown: no chronic-use studies in healthy people. What months or years of repeated GH pulses plus appetite surges does is entirely uncharted.

Interactions

Unknown: no drug-interaction studies, no data in pregnancy, no data in people with diabetes, pituitary disorders, or cancer. The label for these questions is blank.

Plain English

Known: banned in sport, vials unregulated, hunger guaranteed-ish. Theoretical: the hunger backfires into fat gain, and the hormone ripples add up. Unknown: almost everything long-term.

07Head to head

GHRP-6 vs the field

GHRP-6 isn't the only way to poke the GH system — and for most goals, it's the hungriest way. Here's how it stacks up against the neighbors that share its territory, on the dimensions that actually differ: receptor behavior, GH response, appetite, duration, and evidence.

GHRP-6 vs GHRP-2

Ghrelin-receptor activityBoth are full ghrelin-receptor agonists. GHRP-2 is often described as stronger per-microgram on GH release — but head-to-head human data is thin.
GH responseBoth trigger sharp GH pulses. GHRP-2's pulse is frequently reported as larger; neither has outcome data.
Appetite effectsGHRP-6 is the hunger king — GHRP-2 also stimulates appetite but is generally described as less ravenous.
DurationBoth short-acting pulses, minutes to a couple of hours. No contest.
EvidenceBoth: lab-measured GH pulses, zero proven health benefits in healthy people.

GHRP-6 vs Ipamorelin

Ghrelin-receptor activityIpamorelin hits the same receptor but far more selectively — the key design difference.
GH responseComparable GH pulses in studies; ipamorelin is often called the cleaner pulse.
Appetite effectsThis is the split: ipamorelin causes little to no hunger. GHRP-6's appetite surge is its defining trait.
DurationBoth short pulses. Ipamorelin's claim is selectivity, not longevity.
EvidenceSame story both: GH pulses measured, benefits unproven. Ipamorelin also lacks outcome data.

GHRP-6 vs MK-677

Ghrelin-receptor activityMK-677 is an oral ghrelin-receptor agonist — same target, pill form, much longer action.
GH responseMK-677 raises GH/IGF-1 around the clock; GHRP-6 fires brief pulses.
Appetite effectsBoth increase appetite — MK-677's is sustained for as long as it's taken, GHRP-6's comes in waves.
DurationMK-677: ~24-hour effect from one dose. GHRP-6: minutes-to-hours per pulse. Totally different exposure.
EvidenceMK-677 has actual human trials (small, stalled programs); GHRP-6 has lab studies and forums.

GHRP-6 vs CJC-1295

Ghrelin-receptor activityDifferent receptors entirely. CJC-1295 mimics GHRH at the GHRH receptor — no ghrelin activity, no hunger wave.
GH responseBoth end at GH release, via different doors. Stacking them is the classic “two doors” combo.
Appetite effectsCJC-1295: essentially none. GHRP-6: the whole point for some, the dealbreaker for others.
DurationCJC-1295 (no DAC): hours-long elevation. GHRP-6: sharp pulse. With DAC: days vs minutes.
EvidenceCJC-1295 has early human GH/IGF-1 data; neither has proven benefits in healthy users.

GHRP-6 vs CJC + Ipamorelin

Ghrelin-receptor activityThe combo uses ipamorelin's selective ghrelin-receptor hit — deliberately avoiding GHRP-6's hunger sledgehammer.
GH responseTwo pathways at once (GHRH + ghrelin) — the synergy rationale. GHRP-6 alone is one loud pathway.
Appetite effectsThe combo is chosen specifically to dodge GHRP-6-level hunger.
DurationCombo: coordinated pulses over hours. GHRP-6: one sharp spike and a fridge raid.
EvidenceCombo rationale is mechanistic + anecdotal. Same evidence ceiling: no proven outcomes.
Plain English

GHRP-6 is the loudest, hungriest of the GH secretagogues — the one everything newer was designed to be less like. If appetite is the goal, nothing beats it; if appetite is the problem, every alternative exists because of it.

ESTABLISHED · solid scientific data

What the research actually shows

  • A reliable GH pulse. Controlled human studies show GHRP-6 provokes a sharp growth-hormone pulse from the pituitary — the single best-documented effect of the molecule.
  • A reliable appetite surge. Increased hunger and food intake are measured in studies, not just reported on forums — the most replicated finding of all.

PREDICTED / EMERGING · not proven

Reasons to suspect, not reasons to believe

  • Appetite in wasting contexts. If stimulating hunger helps anywhere, it would be where appetite has collapsed — but no trial has shown this in patients. Predicted from the hunger effect, demonstrated nowhere.
  • Body-composition hopes. Growth hormone influences muscle and fat, so a GH pulse could shift body composition in theory. No human study has ever shown GHRP-6 doing this.
  • Animal-only leads. Some animal work hints at tissue-protective or anti-inflammatory effects. Animal results routinely fail to transfer to humans — these are clues, not conclusions.

Think of it this way: a mechanism suggests, a trial settles. The GH pulse is settled; everything else is a suggestion — and animal studies are suggestions from a different species.

10Glossary

Understanding GHRP-6 Terminology

Twelve terms, each with the science and a plain-English translation. Tap any term to expand it.

The scienceGhrelin is a 28-amino-acid peptide hormone produced mainly by the stomach, often called the "hunger hormone." Its levels rise before meals and fall after eating, signaling the brain's appetite circuits that it is time to eat. Beyond hunger, ghrelin is a potent stimulator of growth hormone release, acting on the ghrelin receptor in the hypothalamus and pituitary. GHRP-6 is a synthetic mimic that activates this same receptor, which is why it produces both hunger and GH pulses.

Plain EnglishGhrelin is your stomach's "feed me" message — a hormone that rises before meals and tells your brain you're hungry. But it has a second job: it also tells the pituitary to release growth hormone. GHRP-6 is a lab-made copycat that hits the same receptor, so it triggers both effects at once. That's the whole deal with this peptide: you can't get the GH pulse without the hunger, because both run through the same switch.

The scienceGHS-R1a, the growth hormone secretagogue receptor type 1a, is the receptor that ghrelin and ghrelin mimics activate. It sits in the hypothalamus and pituitary, where its activation triggers growth hormone release, and in appetite circuits, where it drives hunger. "Secretagogue" simply means a substance that causes another substance to be secreted. One receptor, two outcomes — that single lock explains why GHRP-6's GH pulse and its appetite surge always arrive together.

Plain EnglishGHS-R1a is ghrelin's parking spot — a receptor in the brain and pituitary that both ghrelin and GHRP-6 dock into. Turning it on fires off a burst of growth hormone and, at the same time, cranks up hunger. "Secretagogue" is just the technical word for anything that makes the body release something else. One receptor, two effects: that's why the pulse and the appetite always show up as a package deal.

The scienceA hexapeptide is a peptide chain made of exactly six amino acids, the building blocks of proteins. GHRP-6's sequence (His-D-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys) makes it small, cheap to synthesize, and stable enough to reach its targets in the body. Despite its tiny size, it is a full agonist at the ghrelin receptor — potent enough to trigger a robust GH pulse. Six amino acids, one convincing mimic: the whole GHRP class is defined by doing ghrelin's job in a fraction of the size.

Plain EnglishA hexapeptide is six amino-acid building blocks on a string — a mini-protein. GHRP-6's tiny six-bead chain is cheap to make and sturdy enough to survive the trip to its target. Don't let the size fool you: it's a full-strength trigger for the ghrelin receptor, strong enough to force a real GH pulse. Six beads doing the job of a 28-bead hormone — that's the whole trick of this peptide family.

The scienceA GH pulse is a sharp, temporary burst of growth hormone released by the pituitary into the blood, as opposed to a low, steady trickle. The body naturally releases GH in pulses, with the largest ones during deep sleep, and tissues are built to respond to this rhythm. GHRP-6 triggers an artificial pulse by activating the ghrelin receptor on pituitary cells. In research, the pulse is what gets measured — a spike on the lab readout — which is a biomarker of effect, not proof of any health benefit.

Your body does this naturally, with the biggest bursts during deep sleep, and it's the rhythm your tissues are built around. GHRP-6 forces an artificial burst by flipping the ghrelin receptor in the pituitary. The scienceOrexigenic means appetite-stimulating — the opposite of anorexigenic. Ghrelin is the body's primary orexigenic hormone, and it works through hunger circuits in the hypothalamus. GHRP-6 inherits this property completely, since it activates the same receptor; increased hunger is its most widely reported effect. Whether that is a feature or a flaw depends entirely on the user's goals — welcome for someone trying to eat more, the main side effect for everyone else.

Plain EnglishOrexigenic is the technical word for "makes you hungry." The scienceProlactin is a pituitary hormone best known for driving milk production after childbirth, but it also influences mood, metabolism, immune function, and libido. It's one of the reasons researchers kept refining the formula: newer mimics were built, in part, to keep this unwanted guest quieter.

The scienceCortisol is the body's primary glucocorticoid stress hormone, released by the adrenal glands in response to stress, waking, and low blood sugar. It mobilizes energy but also suppresses immune function and, when chronically elevated, breaks down muscle and raises blood sugar. GHRP-6 can cause a modest cortisol rise alongside each GH pulse. Occasional bumps are normal physiology; the unanswered question is what repeated, drug-driven cortisol spikes do over months or years.

Plain EnglishCortisol is your stress hormone — useful in a crisis, harmful when it's high all the time. It frees up energy fast, but chronically elevated cortisol breaks down muscle, raises blood sugar, and dulls immunity. Each GHRP-6 pulse can nudge it upward. One bump is no big deal; the real question — unanswered by any long-term study — is what repeated bumps add up to.

The scienceThe pituitary is a pea-sized gland at the base of the brain, often called the master gland because it controls other hormone glands. Its front lobe releases growth hormone when stimulated by the ghrelin receptor and by GHRH. GHRP-6 acts directly on pituitary somatotroph cells — the GH-making cells — to force a pulse of hormone release. The scienceThe hypothalamus is a small brain region that acts as mission control for hunger, thirst, temperature, and hormone rhythms. It houses the appetite circuits — NPY and AgRP neurons — that respond to ghrelin by driving the urge to eat. GHRP-6 reaches these same circuits, which is why the GH effect and the hunger effect cannot be separated: both run through the hypothalamus. Plain EnglishThe hypothalamus is the brain's control room for hunger, hormones, and basic drives. Deep inside it sit the hunger circuits that ghrelin switches on — and GHRP-6 flips those same switches. The scienceAmino acids come in mirror-image forms: L-amino acids, which life uses to build proteins, and D-amino acids, their reversed twins. Digestive and blood enzymes are shaped to break down L-forms, so peptides containing D-amino acids resist being chewed up and survive longer. GHRP-6's sequence includes D-tryptophan and D-phenylalanine — this "armor" is a big reason a six-amino-acid peptide can reach its target intact. It is standard medicinal chemistry: flip the building blocks so the body's scissors can't cut them.

Plain EnglishAmino acids come in left- and right-handed versions, like mirror images. Your enzymes are built to cut up the normal left-handed ones, so they barely touch the right-handed kind. GHRP-6 sneaks two right-handed building blocks — D-tryptophan and D-phenylalanine — into its chain. That's its armor: the body's digestive scissors can't get a grip, so the peptide survives long enough to do its job.

The scienceDesensitization is the process by which a receptor becomes less responsive after repeated or prolonged stimulation — the cell turns down the volume on a signal it hears too often. It is a well-established principle in pharmacology, seen with many receptor agonists. Whether repeated GHRP-6 use meaningfully desensitizes the ghrelin receptor is debated: forums argue about cycling protocols, but no clinical study has settled it. The concern is real in principle; the evidence for this specific peptide is anecdotal.

Plain EnglishDesensitization is the "boy who cried wolf" of pharmacology — blast a receptor often enough and it starts ignoring you. It's a real, well-documented phenomenon with many drugs. With GHRP-6 specifically, though, nobody has proven it happens: the cycling debates live in forums, not in studies. So treat it as a plausible concern, not an established fact.

No trial shows GHRP-6 builds muscle, burns fat, or slows aging. The evidence file ends at 'it moves hormones and appetite.'

🔬

A research tool, not a therapy

Its real career is in laboratories, where it helped map the ghrelin system. Medicine never picked it up.

🧪

Newer analogs exist for a reason

GHRP-2 and hexarelin were both built to keep the pulse while trimming the baggage — hunger, prolactin, cortisol.

💬

Stories aren't data

Forum reports are vivid and unverified, tangled with stacking, diet changes, and the placebo-prone nature of 'feeling' GH.

⚖️

The hunger backfire

For physique goals, a drug that reliably triggers ravenous eating can work directly against the user.

🚫

Banned in sport

WADA prohibits GHRPs (S2). Athletes have been sanctioned — the ban doesn't care about the evidence debate.

🏷️

Benefits need tiers

Every benefit claim sits in one of three tiers — shown in humans, predicted/emerging, or community stories — and the tiers are never equal. GHRP-6 has no medical indications; the fit check is general information only.

🔭

What would change the story

A published trial showing a real health outcome — not just a hormone spike. After four decades, none exists.
